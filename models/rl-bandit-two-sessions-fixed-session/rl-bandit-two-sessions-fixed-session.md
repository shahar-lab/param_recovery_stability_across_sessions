# rl-bandit-two-sessions-fixed-session

Fitting-only variant of [rl-bandit-two-sessions](../rl-bandit-two-sessions/rl-bandit-two-sessions.md). Data are generated by `models/rl-bandit-two-sessions/rl-bandit-two-sessions.R`.

## Difference from rl-bandit-two-sessions

The session effect on log beta is fixed instead of random. Each session has its own mean, and there is no `sigma_session` / `z_session`:

\[
\log \beta_{ij} = \mu_{\log\beta,j} + \gamma_i + \gamma_{ij}
\]

\(\mu_{\log\beta,j}\) absorbs the generating \(\mu_{\log\beta} + \gamma_j\). With only two sessions, \(\sigma_{\mathrm{session}}\) is weakly identified, and fixing the session means avoids estimating it.

## ICC

\[
\mathrm{ICC}_{\mathrm{consistency}} = \frac{\sigma^2_{\mathrm{subject}}}{\sigma^2_{\mathrm{subject}} + \sigma^2_{\mathrm{subjectXsession}}},
\qquad
\mathrm{ICC}_{\mathrm{agreement}} = \frac{\sigma^2_{\mathrm{subject}}}{\sigma^2_{\mathrm{subject}} + v_{\mathrm{session}} + \sigma^2_{\mathrm{subjectXsession}}}
\]

\(v_{\mathrm{session}}\) (`var_session`) is the variance of the two fixed session means (\(n - 1\) denominator, as in `irr::icc`). It describes these two sessions, not a population of sessions, so this agreement ICC targets the sample value, not the population \(\sigma^2_{\mathrm{session}}\).

## Priors (Stan)

- `mu_log_beta[j] ~ normal(1, 1)`
- `sigma_subject`, `sigma_subjectXsession ~ half-normal(0, 1)`
- `mu_alpha ~ normal(0, 1.5)`, `sigma_alpha ~ half-normal(0, 1)`
- `z_subject`, `z_subjectXsession`, `z_alpha ~ std_normal()`
